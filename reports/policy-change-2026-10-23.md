# First test of the CHAOSS policy-change metric

This is a first test of the [CHAOSS AI policy change metric](https://github.com/chaoss/wg-ai-alignment/blob/policy-evolution/metrics/ai-alignment-community-governed-use/ai-use-policy-change.md) on a purposive sample of AI-policy revisions, using source revision dates. Each entry describes a policy change, not a judgment of a project. The analysis also references the [CHAOSS AI policy specificity metric](https://github.com/chaoss/wg-ai-alignment/blob/policy-evolution/metrics/ai-alignment-community-governed-use/ai-use-consent-policy-specificity.md) to identify the domains involved.

The labels “tightening” and “loosening” are interpretive and open to debate. The entries distinguish first-observed policy baselines, substantive rule changes, and clarifications or reorganizations; direction is assigned only where the evidence supports it.

Provisional coding of the 28 revisions identifies 24 that introduce or revise substantive policy provisions: 4 first-observed baselines and 20 subsequent substantive rule revisions. The remaining 4 clarify, operationalize, or reorganize existing guidance without establishing a substantive rule change. A substantive provision alters a permission, prohibition, obligation, exception, or enforcement rule. Baselines are separated from subsequent revisions because the sample does not establish a prior AI-specific rule for comparison, not because the baseline provisions are non-substantive. These counts and classifications are not independently validated: AI-assisted review can make mistakes, and each should be checked against the source revision and surrounding policy context before being treated as a result.

## Method notes

- List of projects comes from policies and other lists tracked in the CHAOSS moderation/README file
- I used Claude Code to look for policy related to AI in any governance files; and to identify changes in files - and whether or not those mention AI.
- I then reviewed (human in the loop) diffs that came up and tracked as either 'tightening' or 'loosening' as my own evaluation for now.
